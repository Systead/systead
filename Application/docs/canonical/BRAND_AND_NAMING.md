# Brand and naming

**Status:** Canonical working guidance
**Effective:** 19 July 2026

## Locked working names

- **Systead** — parent brand and platform.
- **Systead Core** — generic platform foundation.
- **House** — configured operator environment.
- **AuthorMachine by Systead** — first specialist product.
- **Stokknes House** — private flagship and proving ground.

**Systead.com** is the active primary domain asset for the platform.

## Naming hierarchy

```text
Systead
├── Systead Core
├── House
│   ├── desks and modules
│   └── AuthorMachine by Systead
└── Stokknes House (private proving ground)
```

## Naming rules

1. Write **Systead**, not “SysTead,” “Sys Stead,” or “Systead OS” as the default public name.
2. Write **AuthorMachine** as one word.
3. Use **AuthorMachine by Systead** on first public reference where brand relationship matters.
4. Use **House** for the configured environment concept.
5. “Systead House” may clarify context, but must not become a separate parent product.
6. Do not imply that Stokknes House is what every user receives.
7. Do not use AuthorMachine as the name of generic Systead infrastructure.
8. Use descriptive desk and module names that survive expansion beyond one user or industry.
9. Avoid “OS” unless the product deliberately adopts and can defend that category later.
10. Avoid “AI operating system,” “second brain,” or “life OS” as primary positioning; these are crowded labels and obscure the command, ownership, and recovery model.

## Primary public line

> Keep complicated work together. Keep yourself in charge.

The previous line, “Local-first command systems. Operator-owned, explainable, and reversible,” remains useful as technical shorthand in architecture or repository contexts. It should not lead the public product story.

## Expanded public description

> Systead is being built as a private House for complicated work that has outgrown scattered apps, files, notes, messages, and memory. It keeps context, history, unresolved questions, and next actions connected while leaving important judgment with the person using it.

## Current status line

> Running private pre-alpha · first-week stress test · not yet open for public testing.

Do not use the previous phrase “Alpha foundation in preparation” as the primary status. It understates that a private build exists and runs.

## Hero line

> Keep the work together. Keep yourself in charge.

This is the main public-facing hero line. It speaks to the human problem before the architecture.

“Run the work. Keep the judgment.” may still be used in technical or command-safety material where the authority model is the focus.

## Brand character

Systead should feel:

- calm, capable, and lived-in rather than sterile;
- durable rather than trendy;
- thoughtful rather than corporate-generic;
- technically credible without forcing technical language into every section;
- private by design without sounding paranoid;
- powerful without selling autonomous replacement of human judgment;
- architectural, practical, and humane rather than gadget-like;
- spacious enough to think in;
- clearly distinct from the darker literary atmosphere of Stokknes Publishing.

## Voice layers

Systead uses three levels of language. They should not be mixed indiscriminately.

### 1. Product language

Lead with the ordinary human problem, the lived benefit, and what using the product should feel like.

Prefer:

> Open the House and know what changed, what matters now, and what is waiting for your judgment.

Avoid leading with:

> Canonical entities are resolved through reviewable command contracts.

### 2. Feature language

Explain what the product can do using concrete categories, outcomes, and examples. Technical terms may appear when they help the reader understand a real capability.

Prefer:

> Keep a project connected to its files, decisions, source material, open questions, and next safe action.

### 3. System language

Use the heavier vocabulary for architecture, safety, implementation, and developer documentation. This is where terms such as canonical records, provenance, command lifecycle, adapters, schema migration, rollback, and audit belong.

Prefer:

> Material actions declare destination, affected records, risk, approval requirement, verification, and recovery path.

Public copy should still be direct, concrete, honest about maturity, free of exaggerated futurism, and understandable without software-architecture expertise.

## Readiness language

Allowed current terms:

- running private pre-alpha;
- private stress test;
- public-safe interface study;
- synthetic data;
- provisional architecture;
- implementation bootstrap;
- not open for testing;
- no release date announced.

Terms requiring evidence or a later status decision:

- alpha;
- beta;
- early access;
- production-ready;
- secure;
- stable;
- enterprise-ready;
- fully private;
- autonomous;
- open source;
- available soon.

## Visual direction

The public product identity must stand apart from Stokknes Publishing. It should feel like a calm architectural workspace rather than a dark literary house.

Working public palette and materials:

- warm paper and pale mineral backgrounds;
- spruce or deep green as the structural brand colour;
- restrained clay or copper accents for warmth and status;
- charcoal technical sections only when the content becomes technical;
- fine grid, drafting, ledger, or field-note references;
- strong typography and generous negative space;
- minimal decorative motion;
- no gothic, noir, smoky, distressed, manuscript-cover, or author-persona styling.

Layout rules:

- public sections should breathe and avoid stacking many equal cards;
- categories should often use wide editorial rows rather than cramped dashboards;
- product copy should have a readable measure and visible hierarchy;
- dense feature grids belong lower on the page;
- wide interface images must be capped, framed, and allowed to sit inside generous margins;
- secondary screenshots may form a two-column pair on desktop and stack on mobile;
- the page should feel owned and coherent even when no screenshot is present.

The application itself may remain darker or more operational where that improves focus. Public brand presentation and application UI do not need to use identical surface colours.

## Logo status

Current marks remain candidates and are not legally or visually locked.

Before final selection, review:

- originality and similarity risk;
- trademark and design clearance;
- small-size and favicon legibility;
- monochrome use;
- app-icon use;
- light and dark backgrounds;
- relation to, but distinction from, the Stokknes Publishing House identity;
- reproducibility without relying on a specific font file.

## Public image labels

Any reconstructed or synthetic pre-alpha image should use language such as:

> Pre-alpha interface study · synthetic data · UI subject to change.

Do not label reconstructed studies as “live screenshots.” Real screenshots may be used later only after private-data review and deliberate approval.
