## Incorporation trigger

Do not change legal ownership notices to Systead Systems S.L.U. merely because incorporation is planned.

The company becomes the relevant legal entity only after the required formation steps and registration have occurred. Before registration, actions purportedly taken in the company’s name may create personal responsibility for the people taking them.

## Transfer options after incorporation

Once Systead Systems S.L.U. legally exists, choose and document one of the following for pre-incorporation IP:

### Option A — assignment

Marius transfers specified economic rights and assets to Systead Systems S.L.U.

This should be written, specific, and supported by a schedule identifying the transferred assets, rights, territories, duration, and any retained rights.

### Option B — exclusive licence

Marius keeps ownership but grants Systead Systems S.L.U. exclusive rights to use and commercialize specified assets.

This may be useful as an interim structure but still requires clear written terms.

### Option C — limited or non-exclusive licence

Marius keeps ownership and grants the company narrower use rights.

This provides flexibility but may be less clean for investors, buyers, partners, or future due diligence.

The commercial, tax, accounting, valuation, and liability consequences should be reviewed before choosing.

## Moral rights and attribution

The creator’s moral rights and authorship position should be respected even if economic exploitation rights are later transferred or licensed.

Public company ownership and personal authorship are not the same statement.

## Origin and evidence register

Maintain evidence of creation and ownership through:

- Git commit history;
- dated design exports;
- domain purchase records;
- repository ownership records;
- invoices and tool subscriptions;
- source files;
- decision register entries;
- contributor agreements;
- third-party licence records;
- an IP inventory updated before incorporation and before any transfer.

## Contributor rule

Before accepting material contributions, define in writing:

- whether the contribution is assigned or licensed;
- whether the contribution may be redistributed;
- whether the contributor used third-party or employer-owned material;
- whether generated assets have tool-specific restrictions;
- whether the contribution can be transferred to a future Systead company.

A public repository without a public licence does not automatically grant broad reuse rights.
